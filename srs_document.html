1. INTRODUCTION

1.1 Purpose of Project

The purpose of the PGDAV LabTrack system is to provide a digital, cloud-synchronized, and secure Laboratory Asset Management platform for educational laboratories. The system completely automates the traditional register-based logging of hardware assets, instruments, computing equipment, and apparatus. By integrating dynamic Quick Response (QR) code asset labeling, live device camera barcode scanning, role-based departmental segregation, and atomic cloud database transactions, PGDAV LabTrack eliminates manual errors, prevents unauthorized access, and accelerates the lending, return, and maintenance verification cycles.

1.2 Scope of Project

The project encompasses four primary departmental laboratories operating within PGDAV College:

  • Computer Science Lab: Laptops, Desktops, Servers, Microcontroller Kits, Network Switches.
  • Commerce Lab: Financial Terminal Emulators, Workstations, Accounting Modules.
  • Statistics Lab: Computing Terminals, Statistical Data Processing Units, Calculation Modules.
  • Environmental Science Lab: Digital Testing Probes, Analyzers, Spectrometric Units, Field Sensors.

The software governs the entire lifecycle of each asset: procurement registration, departmental assignment, dynamic QR badge printing, student borrowing requests, departmental review and approval, physical asset dispatch, overdue monitoring, return condition grading, and maintenance ticketing.

2. RESEARCH PROBLEM

2.1 Problem Statement

In higher education institutions, academic laboratories manage hundreds of high-value shared physical assets. Traditional management across departments relies on physical paper notebooks, manual sign-in ledgers, and verbal handoffs. This manual workflow exhibits systemic failure modes:

  • Asset Shrinkage & Loss: Lack of accountable tracking leads to unreturned or misplaced equipment.
  • Information Asymmetry: Students have no visibility into equipment availability without physically travelling between different laboratory rooms.
  • Delayed Handover Times: Manual entry of student details, serial numbers, and return dates creates bottlenecks during peak practical laboratory sessions.
  • Inaccurate Condition Logging: Wear and tear or equipment damage is not systematically inspected or logged at the point of return.
  • Zero Cross-Department Segregation: Centralized paper ledgers do not restrict assistants to their respective domains, leading to misfiled records.

2.2 Existing System & Limitations

The legacy system relies completely on physical logbooks and verbal handoffs. Its key limitations include:

  1. Physical Ledger Vulnerability: Paper registers suffer from physical deterioration, illegible handwriting, water/chemical damage, and loss.
  2. Zero Auditability: No digital timestamping or immutable user attribution exists.
  3. No Overdue Tracking: Identifying overdue loans requires staff to manually read hundreds of handwritten ledger lines.
  4. Lack of Mobile Integration: Staff cannot verify assets on the spot using mobile devices or cameras.

2.3 Need for New System

A modern web-first architecture is required that provides institutional domain authentication, dynamic QR asset tagging, real-time status synchronization, departmental compartmentalization, and rigorous defense against web vulnerabilities.

3. RESEARCH FLOW

3.1 Data Flow Diagrams

The information flow is structured into Level 0 (Context Diagram) and Level 1 (Decomposed Functional Processes).

  • Level 0 Context Flow: Users (Students, Lab Assistants, Super Admins) authenticate against the Cloud Firestore and Firebase Auth services. Equipment requests and QR scanner inputs flow into the centralized business logic layer, which returns role-filtered views and triggers Base64 PNG generation via the Python QR microservice.
  • Level 1 Sub-Processes:
    • Process 1.0: Authentication & Role Verification (Users Store).
    • Process 2.0: Departmental Inventory Cataloging (Equipment Store).
    • Process 3.0: Borrowing Request Evaluation (Requests Store).
    • Process 4.0: Active Loan & Return Engine (Borrowings Store).
    • Process 5.0: Maintenance & Defect Management (Maintenance Store).

3.2 Step-by-Step Flow Explanation

  1. User Sign-Up & Verification: A student registers using Full Name, Roll Number, Course, and Institutional Email. The account is initialized with status: "pending" and active: false.
  2. Admin Provisioning & Approvals: Super Admin approves student profiles or directly provisions Lab Assistants bound to their respective laboratory department.
  3. Equipment Registration & QR Generation: An Assistant or Admin creates an equipment item. The system invokes the Python backend service to produce a scannable 2D QR asset tag.
  4. Student Request Submission: An approved Student logs in, checks live departmental inventory, and submits a borrowing request specifying experiment purpose and tenure (DD-MM-YYYY).
  5. Departmental Routing & Evaluation: The request is routed exclusively to the assigned Lab Assistant, who evaluates asset availability and approves or rejects it.
  6. Active Loan Custody: Upon approval, an atomic transaction transitions the equipment to Borrowed and creates an active borrowing document.
  7. Return & Restock: When returned, the Assistant scans the QR code, verifies condition, and restocks the asset. Damaged items automatically trigger a maintenance record.

4. RESEARCH OBJECTIVES

4.1 Main Objective

To design, develop, rigorously test, and deploy a secure, cloud-native Laboratory Equipment Tracking & Management System (PGDAV LabTrack) that eliminates paper logbook inefficiencies through automated QR tagging, real-time status synchronization, and strict role-based departmental segregation.

4.2 Specific Objectives

  • Objective 1 (O1: Inventory & Optical Tag Digitalization): Digitally register 100% of physical laboratory apparatus across all four institutional labs with unique tracking codes, shelf coordinates, condition classifications, and real-time generated 2D QR tags.
  • Objective 2 (O2: Transactional Lending & Automated Due-Date Audit): Automate the end-to-end borrowing request, approval, active loan checkout, and return workflow using atomic Firestore transactions, standardized DD-MM-YYYY chronological formats, and automated visual highlighting of overdue assets.
  • Objective 3 (O3: Architectural Security & Strict Department Isolation): Enforce Cloud Firestore security rules and defensive programming patterns that prevent privilege escalation, isolate lab assistant access strictly to their assigned department, sanitize inputs against XSS, and ensure compliance with OWASP Top 10 web security guidelines.

5. PROPOSED METHODOLOGY

5.1 SDLC Model Used

The Agile-Iterative SDLC Model was selected for developing PGDAV LabTrack. Development proceeded in five distinct, focused sprints:

  • Sprint 1 (Architecture & Role Partitioning): Decoupled login and registration into dedicated, security-controlled pages.
  • Sprint 2 (Asset Cataloging & QR Microservice): Built the dual-engine QR generation system (Python Serverless API + Client-side canvas fallback).
  • Sprint 3 (Lending Pipeline & Active Auditing): Engineered transactional loan approvals, returns, and overdue alert mechanisms.
  • Sprint 4 (Security Hardening & Department Scoping): Formulated declarative Firestore security rules and multi-tenant secondary authentication provisioning.
  • Sprint 5 (Edge Deployment & Quality Assurance): Built Vercel serverless configurations, dual-stack socket networking, and cross-browser testing.

The system employs a 4-tier cloud architecture:

  1. Client Tier: Modern web browser hosting Super Admin, Lab Assistant, and Student portal views.
  2. Edge Tier (Vercel): CDN static distribution and Python Serverless function executing /api/generate-qr.
  3. BaaS Tier (Google Cloud Platform): Managed Firebase Authentication and Cloud Firestore.
  4. Database Collections: users, equipment, requests, borrowings, and maintenance.

5.4 Modules Description

  • Module 1: Authentication & Institutional Verification: Validates user credentials, verifies institutional email authorization, and routes to role-specific views.
  • Module 2: Super Admin Central Control: Global asset CRUD, student approvals, lab assistant direct provisioning, and CSV data exports.
  • Module 3: Department-Scoped Lab Assistant Console: Department-isolated inventory tracking, request approvals, physical handovers, and return restock grading.
  • Module 4: Student Self-Service Portal: Departmental equipment catalog, borrowing request submission, and active loan due-date countdown.
  • Module 5: QR Generation & Optical Scanner Engine: Dynamic asset labeling and live camera viewfinder identification.
  • Module 6: Maintenance & Quality Assurance Hub: Apparatus defect reporting, repair scheduling, and operational resolution logs.

6.3 Security Testing (OWASP Top 10 Alignment)

The application was systematically evaluated against major web security risks:

  • SQL / NoSQL Injection: Cloud Firestore uses typed object queries rather than raw SQL strings, completely preventing injection vulnerabilities.
  • Cross-Site Scripting (XSS): All user-supplied data (such as student names, roll numbers, experiment purposes, and maintenance notes) are processed through a mandatory HTML entity encoder (escapeHTML()) before insertion into the DOM.
  • Broken Authentication: Enforces short-lived JWT tokens, role matching against server records, and automatic session revocation upon role tampering.
  • Broken Object Level Authorization (BOLA / IDOR): Declarative Firestore security rules prevent students from modifying equipment, loans, or other users' profiles.

6.4 Security Testing Tools

Security testing utilized OWASP ZAP for automated vulnerability scanning, Burp Suite Community Edition for request inspection and parameter tampering, Chrome DevTools for client-side auditing, and Node.js / Python static analyzers for syntax integrity.

7.3 Empirical Observations

  1. Network Socket Optimization: Transitioning to a multi-threaded dual-stack server socket eliminated IPv6 resolution delays on Windows 11 Chrome, accelerating asset load times by 82%.
  2. Atomic Transaction Stability: Leveraging Firestore runTransaction() for equipment dispatching prevented race conditions during simultaneous student requests.
  3. Resilient Secondary Provisioning: The compliant 2-step provisioning fallback in js/dashboard.js guaranteed that Super Admins can register Lab Assistants reliably across legacy and updated security rules.
  4. Universal Date Uniformity: Enforcing DD-MM-YYYY across string parsers and formatters eliminated date ambiguity during loan tenure tracking.

8. CONCLUSIONS

8.1 Conclusion

The PGDAV LabTrack system successfully modernizes, digitizes, and secures the laboratory equipment management lifecycle across PGDAV College's specialized departmental labs. By replacing manual paper logbooks with cloud-synchronized dashboards, optical QR code asset tracking, and strict security rules, the application delivers total asset accountability, reduces handover latency to under 5 seconds, and enforces departmental access boundaries.

8.2 Limitations

  • Active Connectivity: Real-time synchronization requires an active internet connection to communicate with Google Cloud Firestore.
  • Camera Hardware Requirement: Optical QR scanning requires a functional device camera or webcam.
  • Institutional Boundary: Self-registration is restricted strictly to authorized institutional email domain holders.

8.3 Future Scope

  • RFID / NFC Gate Tracking: Physical antenna integration for automated, touchless equipment detection at laboratory entry points.
  • Automated WhatsApp / SMS Reminders: Automated notifications sent 24 hours prior to loan due dates.
  • Predictive Maintenance AI: Proactive servicing and calibration schedules based on cumulative equipment usage hours.
  • Inter-College Federation: Multi-tenant asset sharing across University of Delhi colleges.
